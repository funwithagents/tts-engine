## What stood out

The "× real time" figures below are `speed_x` (audio over the time after the first chunk), in `results.csv`. The summary's real-time × counts the whole call, so it reads lower, most of all for backends that deliver a whole sentence at a time.

- Local tiny models (phonon, pocket 2m–18m) answer in 9–35 ms, about 2–9× faster than the fastest API; stream TTFA stays within 8 ms of say TTFA for them, so the measurement isolates the module.
- Fastest APIs: Cartesia at 83–86 ms once its socket is held (it was 203–206 per utterance), then ElevenLabs flash_v2_5, v3_conversational and v4_turbo at 112–136, gradium at 154–165, Deepgram at 174–213 (was 394–516), the openai-compatible 2025-03-20 snapshot at 367.
- Non-streaming backends show a TTFA that grows with the text and 0.5–2.1 s gaps between chunks: ElevenLabs multilingual_v2 over HTTP, kokoro, piper `high`, openai-compatible `tts-1`/`tts-1-hd`. A gap is not always a stall: on the three-sentence stream an earlier sentence's audio covers kokoro's and `tts-1`'s gaps, while multilingual_v2 still runs out for about 300 ms.
- TTFA counts the first bytes, silent or not, so it flatters a backend that opens with silence. Deepgram Flux reaches first audio before Aura-2 (174 vs 213 ms) but its first voiced chunk arrives at 820 ms against 366 (TTFV in `results.csv`, this voice), after about 950 ms of leading silence, and it generates slower (1.5× vs 2.2× real time). That answers the per-utterance question: Aura-2 stays the better default. ElevenLabs v3/v4 reach first audio about 5× later than their conversational/turbo siblings, generate about 2–3× slower, and stall; qwen3 0.6B opens with at least 320 ms of silence, its first voiced chunk arriving about 130–190 ms after its first chunk.
- Deepgram's v1 models (`aura-*`) show a 500–700 ms outlier about every 16 flushes: the module replaces the socket at its budget of 16 flushes a minute (v1 allows 20), which back-to-back runs reach far sooner than ordinary speech. The medians are clean; the p90 includes it. Flux, on v2, has no such budget and no such outliers.
- `deepgram/aura-asteria-en`'s stream TTFA of 109 ms is not a streaming gain: its TTFA moves between about 105, 158 and 210 ms each time the socket is replaced, so the difference between its cells follows the socket, not the mode.
- `mistral/voxtral-mini-tts-3-0`'s headline of 453 ms is its highest cell; the three others read 382–412 ms over the same run. Its first audio arrives as a block of about 400 ms of speech.
- Reproducibility: ElevenLabs flash_v2_5 matched within 15 ms across two runs; the full OpenAI Realtime 2.1 model swings ±0.3 s between runs.
- On the M5, int8 does not pay: pocket `quantize` is slower than fp32 at every size (2m 15 → 18 ms, 7m 17 → 29, 18m 29 → 35, 100M 37 → 75) with a lower real-time factor; phonon 40m on CPU is 19–28 ms against 14–15 on Metal, `q8_0` the best CPU variant (19–22 ms, 45× real time) and `q4k` the slowest and noisiest (22× real time, p90 up to 42 ms). Whether int8 helps on an ARM CPU without Metal is exactly what the Pi result has to show. On the Pi it does: pocket `quantize` is faster at each tiny size measured there, and phonon `q4k` ties or beats `q8_0`.
- Gemini: the flash models reach first audio in 0.5–0.9 s (lite 469 ms, 3.8-flash 849, 3.1-preview 862; 3.8-flash read 691 at n=20 on 2026-10-28, so its headline moves about 150 ms between days), the 2.5-pro preview in 5 s on the short text and 18 s on the three-sentence one. Counting its startup, 2.5-pro delivers below real time on the three-sentence stream (0.92×, five runs). Daily quota, not latency, is what limits measuring them.
- The exception is kokoro, which runs on onnxruntime: int8 halves its TTFA (262 vs 528 ms) and more than doubles its speed (20.4× vs 9.2× real time), while fp16 changes nothing (525 ms). int8 is the kokoro to try on the Pi.

- **CPU and memory** (every local entry, 2026-10-29; CPU in percent of one core, every thread summed). Uncapped, the onnxruntime and ptts CPU paths take every core they find: piper 720–830 %, kokoro 750–815 %, phonon on CPU 600–730 % and its `q4k` variants 930–1020 % (ten cores for a 40M model). phonon on Metal needs 38–49 % and qwen3 on Metal 23–38 %, the GPU doing the work; pocket stays at 143–194 % because its library pins torch to one thread. Peak memory: phonon 250–550 MB, piper 335–565 MB, kokoro 540–870 MB, pocket 440–800 MB for the tiny models, 1.0–1.15 GB for 100M and 2.9 GB for the 24-layer model, qwen3 2.9–5.7 GB. On a Pi with about 2 GB free beside its daemon, that rules out the pocket 24-layer model and every qwen3 before any speed question.
- **Thread caps.** phonon's default pool oversubscribes a 31M model: every cap beats the uncapped row on time to first audio and real-time factor, same day (`31m-en@cpu`: 18 ms / 40× / 603 % uncapped against 12 ms / 48× / 191 % at one thread, 14 / 50× / 249 % at two, 12 / 52× / 348 % at four; `+q8_0`: 14 ms / 50× / 662 % against 10 / 56× / 177 %, 12 / 64× / 239 %, 9 / 61× / 328 %), and one thread still reads 177–191 % of a core, so ptts works outside its tensor pool too. The `q4k` variants gain as well, with one exception (`31m-en@cpu+q4k`: 26 ms uncapped against 9–16 ms capped; `40m-multilingual@cpu+q4k`: 28 ms against 17–18 at two and four threads, but 31 at one). kokoro and piper scale with the threads they get and read about the thread count (107–118 %, 210–231 %, 410–455 %): kokoro fp32 1847 → 1033 → 620 ms at one, two, four threads (528 uncapped), 2.6× → 4.8× → 7.8× real time; int8 434 → 301 ms (262 uncapped) at 12.4× → 17.8×, and no audio at all at one thread; piper ljspeech-medium 184 → 108 → 68 ms (66 uncapped) at 28× → 50× → 86×, lessac-low 115 → 78 → 46 ms (49 uncapped) at 44× → 78× → 127×.
- **What this said for the Pi**, before it was measured (four A72 cores, about 1.5 of them taken by the daemon): phonon `q8_0` at one thread (10 ms, 56× real time on one M5 core, 177 % of a core, 387 MB) and the tiny pocket models (15–17 ms at 160–180 %, 440–570 MB) are the candidates that leave the daemon its cores with margin, even twenty times slower per core; piper lessac-low at one or two threads (115 / 78 ms, 44× / 78×) is the fallback; kokoro int8 needs two threads (434 ms, 12×) and cannot run on one; kokoro fp32 is out (2.6× on one core, 1.8 s to first audio). The Pi result [`20261029-1124_pi4-1500-nodaemon`](../20261029-1124_pi4-1500-nodaemon/findings.md) disproved most of this: a Pi core is far more than twenty times slower for phonon at one thread, phonon needs all four cores to stay above real time, and kokoro int8 takes 15 s to first audio.
- Reproducibility across days, local entries: 19 of the 36 re-measured rows moved by 2 ms or less of TTFA p50 and 27 by 5 ms or less; the largest moves were qwen3 0.6B BF16 (231 → 212 ms) and piper lessac-high (331 → 317).

## What this result changed

- OpenAI Realtime holds its socket across utterances (`440c60e`; [openai spec](../../../specs/modules/openai.md)).
- openai-compatible defaults to the `gpt-4o-mini-tts-2025-03-20` snapshot, and the live row follows it (`c362ca3`, `f69efdf`; [openai-compatible spec](../../../specs/modules/openai-compatible.md)).
- Deepgram keeps `aura-2-thalia-en` as its default over Flux (`c362ca3`), decided on per-utterance numbers. With the held socket Flux leads on TTFA, but only with silence: Aura-2's voice arrives first and it generates faster, so the choice stands.
- The `threads` field on phonon, kokoro and piper (`cfd9e20`; [phonon](../../../specs/modules/phonon.md), [kokoro](../../../specs/modules/kokoro.md), [piper](../../../specs/modules/piper.md) specs) was measured here; a phonon default below its own, since the pool is oversubscribed on a many-core host, was left to the Pi result, which settled it: no default ([its findings](../20261029-1124_pi4-1500-nodaemon/findings.md)).
